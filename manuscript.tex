\begin{abstract}
For every real order $1<\alpha\leq2$, we prove that the reference stabilized
geometric R\'enyi divergence of two finite dimensional deterministic physical
superchannels equals its nested amortized extension. Joint inserted channels
$AR\to BR$ and the external state references in the inner channel divergences
range over all positive finite dimensions. The proof combines a supported
matrix perspective transformer inequality with a comb contraction, a completion
of positive testers, and a physical realization of positive definite testers.
It includes singular supported operators and gives value $+\infty$ for
unsupported comb pairs. In the supported case, the ordinary supremum already
has the same value at reference dimension $|C||A||B|$. A conventional proof is
presented alongside a correspondence with a Lean 4 theorem and a reproducible
source artifact. The result concerns the nested quantity of Hirche's
Eq.~(39); it does not establish collapse of the larger fully amortized
quantity in Eq.~(40).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and scope}
Amortization measures the distinguishability gained by a transformation after
subtracting distinguishability already present in its inputs. For geometric
R\'enyi divergence, Fang and Fawzi established a Choi formula, a channel chain
rule, and the resulting collapse of ordinary channel amortization
\cite[Lemmas~5--7]{FF21}. For superchannels, both a channel and a state can
carry input distinguishability. Hirche distinguishes several corresponding
amortized quantities \cite[Definitions~4.4--4.5]{Hir23}.

We establish equality between the reference stabilized ordinary quantity in
Hirche's Eq.~(33) and the nested amortized quantity in Eq.~(39). The additional
argument beyond ordinary channel collapse is a uniform bound on a tester
obtained by contracting the insertion and state perspectives. Its marginal
slack can be completed to a normalized feasible tester. Conversely, a positive
definite feasible tester can be realized with a single common input and output
reference, using a pure initial state and a globally trace preserving joint
insertion. These two steps turn the comb moment bound into the required
equality of operational suprema.

The QIQCOP record \code{op_1482756b02794495} cites Hirche's question and
suppresses the inserted-reference labels in its superchannel formulas
\cite{Zoo}. We state the reference convention explicitly. Restricting the
insertion to $A\to B$ changes the problem: an external reference attached only
to the output channel's state tests does not supply a reference through the
insertion. The theorem below resolves the intended reference stabilized
nested question. It must not be read as an assertion for that restricted
convention. Nor does it address the fully amortized quantity in Hirche's
Eq.~(40), arbitrary multi-slot combs, or the endpoint $\alpha=1$.

The operator transformer estimate and channel chain-rule machinery are
established ingredients \cite[Lemma~47 and Lemmas~5--7]{FF21}. We give their
supported singular versions in the form used by the formal proof. Our
contribution here is the superchannel tester argument and its complete formal
verification. A bounded search of primary sources did not locate an earlier
proof of this precise equality; this is not an exhaustive priority claim.
The accompanying preparation record describes that search and its limits.

\section{Definitions and the main theorem}
All spaces are complex Hilbert spaces of positive finite dimension. All
logarithms are to base two. A channel is a complex-linear completely positive
trace preserving map. A state is positive semidefinite with trace one. Write
$X\ll Y$ when $\supp X\subseteq\supp Y$ for positive semidefinite operators.
The symbol $Y^+$ denotes the Moore--Penrose inverse, and $Y^{+1/2}$ its positive
square root, zero on the kernel of $Y$.

Fix $1<\alpha\leq2$ and put $\kappa=\alpha-1$. For positive semidefinite $X,Y$,
define the matrix perspective and its moment by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:perspective}
 \G(X,Y)=Y^{1/2}(Y^{+1/2}XY^{+1/2})^\alpha Y^{1/2},
 \qquad \M(X,Y)=\Tr\G(X,Y).
\end{equation}
On supported states the geometric divergence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:state}
 \Dhat_\alpha(\rho\Vert\sigma)=\frac{1}{\kappa}\log_2\M(\rho,\sigma);
 \qquad \Dhat_\alpha(\rho\Vert\sigma)=+\infty\quad\text{if }\rho\not\ll\sigma.
\end{equation}
Although the matrix expression in \eqref{eq:perspective} is defined for all
positive semidefinite pairs, only supported pairs are assigned a finite
divergence by \eqref{eq:state}.

For channels $N,M:P\to Q$, let
\begin{align}
 d_\alpha(N\Vert M)
 &=\sup_{S,\rho_{SP}}\Dhat_\alpha((\id_S\otimes N)(\rho)\Vert
                                  (\id_S\otimes M)(\rho)),\label{eq:channel}\\
 d^A_\alpha(N\Vert M)
 &=\sup_{\substack{S,\rho_{SP},\sigma_{SP}\\
                    \Dhat_\alpha(\rho\Vert\sigma)<\infty}}
 \left\{\Dhat_\alpha((\id_S\otimes N)(\rho)\Vert
                     (\id_S\otimes M)(\sigma))
              -\Dhat_\alpha(\rho\Vert\sigma)\right\}.\label{eq:channelA}
\end{align}
Here and below $S$ ranges over every positive finite dimension. The
finite-cost restriction permits every finite real cost and retains negative
differences. In particular it never subtracts $\infty$ from $\infty$.

A physical deterministic superchannel taking $A\to B$ channels to $C\to D$
channels has a realization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:physical}
 \Theta(N)=Q_\Theta\circ(N\otimes\id_{E_\Theta})\circ P_\Theta,
 \qquad P_\Theta:C\to AE_\Theta,
 \quad Q_\Theta:BE_\Theta\to D,
\end{equation}
where both maps are channels and the inaccessible memory $E_\Theta$ is finite.
Every finite dimensional deterministic physical supermap has such a
realization by the established realization theorem
\cite[Theorem~1, Eq.~(23)]{CDP08}. This representation theorem is cited as
background; it is not a new formal theorem of our artifact. Each superchannel
in a pair may have its own memory and its own realization.

For $N:AR\to BR$, the extended action $\Theta^R(N):CR\to DR$ applies
$P_\Theta\otimes\id_R$, moves $R$ past $E_\Theta$, applies
$N\otimes\id_{E_\Theta}$, and applies $Q_\Theta\otimes\id_R$ after reversing
the corresponding reordering. The insertion acts on the entire $AR$ input;
it need not have product form. Define
\begin{align}
 \U(\Theta_1\Vert\Theta_2)
 &=\sup_{R,\,N:AR\to BR,\,\rho_{CR}}
   \Dhat_\alpha(\Theta_1^R(N)(\rho)\Vert\Theta_2^R(N)(\rho)),\label{eq:ordinary}\\
 \Adiv(\Theta_1\Vert\Theta_2)
 &=\sup_{\substack{R,\,N,M:AR\to BR\\d^A_\alpha(N\Vert M)\in\mathbb R}}
   \left\{d^A_\alpha(\Theta_1^R(N)\Vert\Theta_2^R(M))
                            -d^A_\alpha(N\Vert M)\right\}.\label{eq:nested}
\end{align}
The inserted reference $R$ ranges over all positive finite dimensions. Each
occurrence of $d^A_\alpha$ independently optimizes its external state reference
$S$. The subtracted term in \eqref{eq:nested} is the entire input-channel
supremum, not the cost of a selected state test. These are precisely the
domains of Hirche's Eqs.~(33) and (39) \cite{Hir23}.

\begin{theorem}[Reference stabilized nested collapse]\label{thm:main}
For every $a=|A|,b=|B|,c=|C|,d=|D|>0$, every real $1<\alpha\leq2$, and every
ordered pair of physical deterministic superchannels as in
\eqref{eq:physical},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapse}
 \Adiv(\Theta_1\Vert\Theta_2)=\U(\Theta_1\Vert\Theta_2).
\end{equation}
Equality is in the extended real numbers and has no additional support,
invertibility, common-memory, or channel-collapse hypothesis.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Ordinary reference absorption]\label{prop:absorb}
The ordinary quantity also has the output-channel form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:absorb}
 \U(\Theta_1\Vert\Theta_2)=
 \sup_{R,N:AR\to BR}d_\alpha(\Theta_1^R(N)\Vert\Theta_2^R(N)).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An ordinary state test in \eqref{eq:ordinary} is a channel test with a
one-dimensional $S$, giving one inequality. Conversely, fix a channel test
with references $R,S$ and state $\rho_{SCR}$. Set $R'=R\otimes S$ and, after
the canonical tensor permutations, insert $N'=N\otimes\id_S:AR'\to BR'$.
Tensoring each tooth of \eqref{eq:physical} by $\id_S$ shows, on every matrix,
\[
 \Theta_i^{R'}(N')=\Theta_i^R(N)\otimes\id_S
\]
in the reordered bases, simultaneously for $i=1,2$. Move the state to
$CR'$ using the same permutation. The output pair is the channel-test pair
up to a unitary permutation, preserving both supports and
\eqref{eq:state}. This supplies an ordinary test for every external-reference
channel test and proves the reverse inequality. This argument is independent
of amortization collapse; it is a mathematical correspondence argument, not
a separately registered Lean theorem in this artifact.
\end{proof}

\section{The supported matrix transformer estimate}
\begin{lemma}[Transformer and congruence identities]\label{lem:transform}
If $X,Y\geq0$ and $X\ll Y$, then for every rectangular matrix $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transform}
 KXK^\dagger\ll KYK^\dagger,
 \qquad \G(KXK^\dagger,KYK^\dagger)\leq K\G(X,Y)K^\dagger.
\end{equation}
If $K$ has a two-sided inverse, the operator inequality is an equality and
support inclusion is equivalent before and after congruence. Moreover,
\begin{align}
 \G(X_1\otimes X_2,Y_1\otimes Y_2)
   &=\G(X_1,Y_1)\otimes\G(X_2,Y_2),\label{eq:tensor}\\
 \G(X,X)&=X.\label{eq:self}
\end{align}
The tensor identity holds for positive semidefinite arguments, and the
corresponding tensor pair is supported whenever both factors are.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First consider $\alpha=2$. On a supported pair,
$G_2(X,Y)=XY^+X$. The block matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
 \begin{pmatrix}Y&X\\X&XY^+X\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}Y^{1/2}\\XY^{+1/2}\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}Y^{1/2}\\XY^{+1/2}\end{pmatrix}^{\!\dagger}
 \geq0.
\end{equation}
Here the support relation makes the off-diagonal blocks equal to $X$.
In a positive block matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}Y'&X'\\X'&T'\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
a vector in $\ker Y'$ gives a zero quadratic form on the upper block and
therefore belongs to $\ker X'$. Thus $X'\ll Y'$. Evaluating the block
quadratic form on $(-Y'^+X'v,v)$ gives $T'\geq X'Y'^+X'$.
Apply these facts to the congruence of \eqref{eq:block} by
$\operatorname{diag}(K,K)$ to obtain \eqref{eq:transform} at order two,
including singular denominators.

For $1<\alpha<2$, use the positive scalar representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integral}
 x^\alpha=c_\alpha\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-2}\frac{x^2}{x+t}\,dt,
 \qquad c_\alpha=\frac{\sin(\pi(\alpha-1))}{\pi}>0,\quad x\geq0.
\end{equation}
For $x>0$ substitution $t=xu$ and the beta integral give this identity; for
$x=0$ both sides vanish. The integrand is $O(t^{\alpha-2})$ near zero and
$O(t^{\alpha-3})$ at infinity, hence is integrable in the stated range.
Diagonalize $Z=Y^{+1/2}XY^{+1/2}$. On $\supp Y$, $Y$ is invertible and
\[
 X(X+tY)^+X=Y^{1/2}Z(Z+tI)^{-1}ZY^{1/2},\qquad t>0.
\]
Both sides vanish on the complementary kernel. Applying
\eqref{eq:integral} to each eigenvalue of $Z$ therefore gives the integrable
quadratic-form identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrixintegral}
 v^\dagger\G(X,Y)v
 =c_\alpha\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-2}
                v^\dagger X(X+tY)^+Xv\,dt.
\end{equation}
This is a finite sum of the integrable scalar expressions just considered.

For every $t>0$, $X\ll X+tY$. Apply the order-two transformer estimate to
$(X,X+tY)$; its congruent denominator is $KXK^\dagger+tKYK^\dagger$.
Integrating its quadratic forms against the positive weight in
\eqref{eq:matrixintegral} proves \eqref{eq:transform}. Support preservation
already follows from the block argument. For invertible $K$, applying the
inequality again with $K^{-1}$ gives the reverse operator inequality;
the same reverse congruence proves support equivalence.

Finally, spectral calculus on tensor-product eigenbases gives
$(Y_1\otimes Y_2)^{+1/2}=Y_1^{+1/2}\otimes Y_2^{+1/2}$ and
$(Z_1\otimes Z_2)^\alpha=Z_1^\alpha\otimes Z_2^\alpha$, proving
\eqref{eq:tensor}. For equal arguments the middle sandwich is the support
projection of $X$, whose $\alpha$th power is itself, proving
\eqref{eq:self}. Tensor support inclusion follows, for example, by writing
$X_i=Y_iZ_i'$ and tensoring these factorizations.
\end{proof}

This transformer statement is the established estimate of
Fang--Fawzi \cite[Lemma~47]{FF21}; the preceding proof follows the Lean
construction through block positivity and the all-real-order integral.
Lean uses Mathlib's positive representing measure for the scalar power
identity, rather than fixing the explicit density $c_\alpha\,dt$.

\begin{lemma}[Normalization and data processing]\label{lem:dp}
Supported states satisfy $\M(\rho,\sigma)\geq1$ and
$\Dhat_\alpha(\rho\Vert\rho)=0$. For every channel $N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dp}
 \Dhat_\alpha((\id_S\otimes N)(\rho)\Vert(\id_S\otimes N)(\sigma))
 \leq\Dhat_\alpha(\rho\Vert\sigma),\qquad d^A_\alpha(N\Vert N)=0.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Diagonalize $Z=\sigma^{+1/2}\rho\sigma^{+1/2}$. The diagonal entries of
$\sigma$ in this eigenbasis are nonnegative weights summing to one, and
their weighted average of the eigenvalues of $Z$ is $\Tr\rho=1$.
Scalar convexity of $x^\alpha$ gives $\M(\rho,\sigma)\geq1$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:self} gives the assertion on equal states.

Use the input-first unnormalized channel Choi matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:choi}
 (J_N)_{(i,\beta),(j,\gamma)}=N(\ket{i}\bra{j})_{\beta\gamma},
 \qquad J_N\geq0,\quad\Tr_QJ_N=I_P.
\end{equation}
The amplified action on $Z'$ has entries
\[
 [\mathcal C(J_N,Z')]_{(\ell,\beta),(\ell',\gamma)}
 =\sum_{i,j}(J_N)_{(i,\beta),(j,\gamma)}Z'_{(\ell,i),(\ell',j)}.
\]
It is a congruence of $J_N\otimes Z'$, obtained by linking the repeated
input labels. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:transform}, \eqref{eq:tensor}, and
\eqref{eq:self} to $(J_N\otimes\rho,J_N\otimes\sigma)$. The output pair is
supported, and its moment is at most
$\Tr\mathcal C(J_N,\G(\rho,\sigma))=\Tr\G(\rho,\sigma)$, using
$\Tr_QJ_N=I_P$. Taking logarithms proves \eqref{eq:dp} on finite-cost
pairs; infinite input divergence makes the inequality automatic. Every
admissible term in $d^A_\alpha(N\Vert N)$ is therefore at most zero, while
equal input states supply the value zero. This proves its asserted value.
\end{proof}

\section{The full finite input-channel cost}
\begin{lemma}[Choi moment bound from the entire cost]\label{lem:cost}
Let $N,M:P\to Q$, with $p=|P|>0$, and suppose
$d^A_\alpha(N\Vert M)=t\in\mathbb R$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:costbound}
 J_N\ll J_M,\qquad
 \Tr_Q\G(J_N,J_M)\leq 2^{\kappa t}I_P.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every invertible $K$ on $P$ with $\Tr K^\dagger K=1$, use the equal pure
input states $\phi_K=\ket{\phi_K}\bra{\phi_K}$, where
$\ket{\phi_K}=(K\otimes I_P)\sum_i\ket{i}\ket{i}$. Their input divergence
is zero, so their output divergence is at most the full supremum $t$.
The two output states are
$(K\otimes I_Q)J_N(K^\dagger\otimes I_Q)$ and the corresponding expression
for $J_M$. The choice $K=I_P/\sqrt p$ and finiteness of $t$ force
$J_N\ll J_M$ by invertible congruence.

Set $F=\G(J_N,J_M)$ and $E=\Tr_QF$. Congruence equality from
Lemma~\ref{lem:transform} gives, for every such $K$,
\[
 \Tr(EK^\dagger K)=\M((\id\otimes N)(\phi_K),(\id\otimes M)(\phi_K))
                       \leq2^{\kappa t}.
\]
Every positive definite trace-one $\eta$ has the form $K^\dagger K$ for an
invertible $K$. For a unit vector $v$, apply this trace bound to
$\eta_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)\ket v\bra v+\varepsilon I_P/p$ and
let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. It follows that
$v^\dagger Ev\leq2^{\kappa t}$ for every $v$, which is exactly
\eqref{eq:costbound}. No channel amortization-collapse assumption was used.
\end{proof}

\section{Comb contractions and tester completion}
Fix computational bases and order the comb space as $C\otimes A\otimes
B\otimes D$. Define $J_\Theta$ from the two teeth of
\eqref{eq:physical} by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comb}
 (J_\Theta)_{(j,i,k,\delta),(j',i',k',\delta')}
 =\sum_{e,f}
 (J_{P_\Theta})_{(j,i,e),(j',i',f)}
 (J_{Q_\Theta})_{(k,e,\delta),(k',f,\delta')}.
\end{equation}
This is positive semidefinite: using Kraus operators $p_h,q_g$ of the two
teeth, it is $\sum_{h,g}\ket{v_{hg}}\bra{v_{hg}}$ with amplitudes
$v_{hg}(j,i,k,\delta)=\sum_e(p_h)_{i e,j}(q_g)_{\delta,k e}$.

Write $x=(j,i,k)\in C\otimes A\otimes B$. Given an insertion reference $R$
and state reference $S$, define the trilinear contraction
\begin{align}\label{eq:contraction}
 &[\mathcal L_{r,s}(J,F,H)]_{(\ell,\delta,t),(\ell',\delta',t')}
 \\
 &\qquad=\sum_{x,y,u,v}
 J_{(x,\delta),(y,\delta')}
 F_{(i,u;k,t),(i',v;k',t')}
 H_{(\ell,j,u),(\ell',j',v)},\notag
\end{align}
where $y=(j',i',k')$, $u,v,t,t'$ are reference labels in $R$, and
$\ell,\ell'$ are labels in $S$. Expanding the two teeth and the inserted
channel on matrix units gives the full action identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:action}
 (\id_S\otimes\Theta^R(N))(\rho)
                 =\mathcal L_{r,s}(J_\Theta,J_N,\rho).
\end{equation}
It holds for every matrix and every complex-linear insertion. In particular
it is not limited to product states or product insertions.

For completeness, \eqref{eq:contraction} is a congruence
$K_{r,s}(J\otimes F\otimes H)K_{r,s}^\dagger$, where
\[
 K_{r,s}=\sum_{\ell,\delta,t}\sum_{j,i,k,u}
 \ket{\ell,\delta,t}
 (\bra{j,i,k,\delta}\otimes\bra{i,u;k,t}\otimes\bra{\ell,j,u}).
\]
Thus, for three supported positive pairs, Lemma~\ref{lem:transform} gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:combchain}
 &\M(\mathcal L(J_1,J_N,\rho),\mathcal L(J_2,J_M,\sigma))
 \\
 &\qquad\leq\Tr\mathcal L(\G(J_1,J_2),\G(J_N,J_M),\G(\rho,\sigma)),\notag
\end{align}
and also gives support inclusion for the contracted pair.

For $W,\Gamma$ on $C\otimes A\otimes B$ use the full-transpose pairing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairing}
 \pair{W}{\Gamma}=\Re\Tr(W\Gamma^{\mathsf T})
                     =\Re\sum_{x,y}W_{xy}\Gamma_{xy}.
\end{equation}
This uses the transpose of the entire tester in the fixed basis, not a
partial transpose. If $W,\Gamma\geq0$ it is nonnegative; if
$\Gamma'\geq\Gamma$ it is monotone. Call $\Gamma$ feasible if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feasible}
 \Gamma\geq0,\qquad\Tr_B\Gamma=\tau_C\otimes I_A,
 \qquad\tau_C\geq0,\quad\Tr\tau_C=1.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Tester completion]\label{lem:tester}
Let $J,F,H\geq0$, put $W=\Tr_DJ$, and suppose
\[
 \Tr_{BR}F\leq qI_{AR},\quad q>0,\qquad\Tr H=m>0.
\]
If $\pair{W}{\Gamma}\leq Q$ for every feasible tester, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tracebound}
 \Tr\mathcal L_{r,s}(J,F,H)\leq qmQ.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Define the contracted tester $T$ on $C\otimes A\otimes B$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tester}
 T_{(j,i,k),(j',i',k')}=
 \sum_{t,\ell,u,v}F_{(i,u;k,t),(i',v;k',t)}
                       H_{(\ell,j,u),(\ell,j',v)}.
\end{equation}
For each $t,\ell$ this is a congruence of $F\otimes H$, linking the
reference label, and summing these congruences proves $T\geq0$.
Taking the trace in \eqref{eq:contraction} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tracepair}
 \Tr\mathcal L_{r,s}(J,F,H)=\pair{W}{T}.
\end{equation}
Both traces here are real for the positive arguments.

Let $Y=\Tr_{SR}H$, a positive operator on $C$ with $\Tr Y=m$.
The $B$ marginal of $T$ is obtained by the same positive contraction from
$\Tr_{BR}F$ and $H$. More explicitly, the relevant map sends $E$ on $AR$ to
the matrix on $CA$ with entries
\[
 \mathcal B_H(E)_{(j,i),(j',i')}=
 \sum_{\ell,u,v}E_{(i,u),(i',v)}H_{(\ell,j,u),(\ell,j',v)}.
\]
For $E\geq0$, its value is a sum of congruences of $E\otimes H$, hence is
positive. It satisfies
$\mathcal B_H(\Tr_{BR}F)=\Tr_BT$ and
$\mathcal B_H(I_{AR})=Y\otimes I_A$. Consequently
\[
 \Delta=q(Y\otimes I_A)-\Tr_BT\geq0.
\]
Complete and normalize the tester by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completion}
 \widetilde\Gamma=\frac{T+\Delta\otimes I_B/b}{qm}.
\end{equation}
It is positive, its $B$ marginal is $(Y/m)\otimes I_A$, and $Y/m$ is a
state, so it is feasible. Since the added slack is positive,
$\pair{W}{T}\leq qm\pair{W}{\widetilde\Gamma}\leq qmQ$.
Together with \eqref{eq:tracepair} this proves the bound for all $r,s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform bound for nested amortization]\label{prop:upper}
Suppose $J_1=J_{\Theta_1}\ll J_2=J_{\Theta_2}$ and set
$W_\alpha=\Tr_D\G(J_1,J_2)$. If $Q>0$ bounds
$\pair{W_\alpha}{\Gamma}$ for every feasible tester, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper}
 \Adiv(\Theta_1\Vert\Theta_2)\leq\kappa^{-1}\log_2Q.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix an admissible pair $N,M:AR\to BR$ and write its entire cost as
$t=d^A_\alpha(N\Vert M)\in\mathbb R$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:cost},
$J_N\ll J_M$ and $F=\G(J_N,J_M)$ has output marginal at most
$qI_{AR}$, with $q=2^{\kappa t}$. Fix any admissible inner test
$\rho,\sigma$ on $SCR$. Its finite cost gives $\rho\ll\sigma$, and
$H=\G(\rho,\sigma)$ has trace $m=\M(\rho,\sigma)\geq1$.
Equations~\eqref{eq:action} and \eqref{eq:combchain}, followed by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tester}, give output support inclusion and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform}
 \M((\id_S\otimes\Theta_1^R(N))(\rho),
     (\id_S\otimes\Theta_2^R(M))(\sigma))
       \leq 2^{\kappa t}\M(\rho,\sigma)Q.
\end{equation}
The supported output states have moment at least one. Taking logarithms
therefore yields
\[
 \Dhat_\alpha(\mathrm{output}_1\Vert\mathrm{output}_2)
          -\Dhat_\alpha(\rho\Vert\sigma)
 \leq t+\kappa^{-1}\log_2Q.
\]
Take the inner supremum, subtract exactly $t$, then take the outer
supremum. This proves \eqref{eq:upper}. All support and reference conditions
used above were obtained from the admissible finite costs.
\end{proof}

\section{Physical realization of testers}
\begin{lemma}[Common-reference realization]\label{lem:realize}
For every positive definite feasible tester $\Gamma$ whose marginal state
$\tau$ is positive definite, there are a reference $R$ of dimension
$r_*=cab$, a channel $N:AR\to BR$, a pure state $\rho_{CR}$, and an invertible
matrix $K_T:CABD\to DR$ such that, for every physical $\Theta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:realizedoutput}
 \Theta^R(N)(\rho)=K_TJ_\Theta K_T^\dagger.
\end{equation}
For supported $J_1\ll J_2$, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:realizedmoment}
 \M(\Theta_1^R(N)(\rho),\Theta_2^R(N)(\rho))
             =\pair{W_\alpha}{\Gamma}\geq1.
\end{equation}
For an unsupported comb pair, the same test has unsupported outputs.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose invertible factors $T,\psi$ with
$\Gamma=TT^\dagger$ and $\tau=\psi\psi^\dagger$. Identify the basis of $R$
with $C\otimes A\otimes B$, and fix labels $i_0,k_0$ to embed
$\iota:C\hookrightarrow R$, $u\mapsto(u,i_0,k_0)$. Prepare
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi}
 \ket\phi=\sum_{j,u}\psi_{ju}\ket j_C\ket{\iota(u)}_R,
 \qquad\rho=\ket\phi\bra\phi.
\end{equation}
Its norm is one because $\Tr\tau=1$.

On the input subspace $A\otimes\iota(C)$, define a matrix $V:AR\to BR$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v}
 V_{(k,t),(i,\iota(u))}=\sum_j(\psi^{-1})_{uj}T_{(j,i,k),t},
\end{equation}
and set it to zero on the orthogonal complement. The marginal equation
for $TT^\dagger$ gives
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{k,t}V_{(k,t),(i,\iota(u))}
             \overline{V_{(k,t),(i',\iota(u'))}}
 &=\delta_{ii'}[\psi^{-1}\tau(\psi^{-1})^\dagger]_{uu'}\\
 &=\delta_{ii'}\delta_{uu'}.
\end{align*}
Thus $V$ is an isometry on that subspace. If $P$ is its input projection and
$\ket{\beta_0}$ a fixed unit vector in $BR$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:insertion}
 N(X)=VXV^\dagger+\Tr((I-P)X)\ket{\beta_0}\bra{\beta_0}.
\end{equation}
The reset term has Kraus operators
$\ket{\beta_0}\bra{i,w}$ for basis vectors outside the embedded subspace.
Their adjoint products sum to $I-P$, while $V^\dagger V=P$. Hence $N$ is
completely positive and trace preserving on the entire input space,
including unused input vectors. The reset term vanishes on the chosen
initial state's reference support and after the preprocessing tooth.

The decisive contraction identity is
\[
 \sum_u V_{(k,t),(i,\iota(u))}\psi_{ju}=T_{(j,i,k),t}.
\]
Insert it and its conjugate into \eqref{eq:contraction}, with $S$ trivial.
This proves \eqref{eq:realizedoutput}, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kt}
 (K_T)_{(\delta,t),(x,\delta')}=\delta_{\delta\delta'}T_{x,t}.
\end{equation}
Since $r_*=cab$, this is, up to a tensor permutation, $T^{\mathsf T}$
tensored with $I_D$. It has a two-sided inverse obtained from
$(T^{-1})^{\mathsf T}$ and the reverse permutation.

For supported $J_1,J_2$, Lemma~\ref{lem:transform} gives perspective equality
under this congruence. Taking traces and summing the output reference label,
\begin{align*}
 \Tr K_T\G(J_1,J_2)K_T^\dagger
 &=\sum_{x,y,\delta,t}\G(J_1,J_2)_{(x,\delta),(y,\delta)}
                                  T_{x,t}\overline{T_{y,t}}\\
 &=\pair{W_\alpha}{TT^\dagger}=\pair{W_\alpha}{\Gamma}.
\end{align*}
Both outputs are normalized physical states, giving the last inequality in
\eqref{eq:realizedmoment}. For an unsupported pair, invertibility of $K_T$
preserves failure of support inclusion, so its output divergence is
$+\infty$. The construction depends on the tester, not on a choice of
superchannel realization, and works simultaneously for both members of
the pair with their separate memories.
\end{proof}

\section{Assembly of the equality and the tester formula}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:dp} shows that equal insertions $N=M$ have entire input cost
zero. For each ordinary state test choose these equal insertions in the
outer amortization and equal states with a trivial external reference in
the inner amortization. Consequently, without any comb support assumption,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 \U(\Theta_1\Vert\Theta_2)\leq\Adiv(\Theta_1\Vert\Theta_2).
\end{equation}

First assume $J_1\ll J_2$ and use $W_\alpha$ as above. Every feasible tester
has trace $a$, so positivity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crude}
 0\leq\pair{W_\alpha}{\Gamma}\leq a\Tr W_\alpha.
\end{equation}
Indeed, for positive $B,P$, $\Tr BP\leq\Tr B\Tr P$, which follows by
diagonalizing $P$ and bounding each eigenvalue by its trace. Apply this
with $P=\Gamma^{\mathsf T}$.

There is a positive definite feasible tester
\begin{equation}\label{eq:star}
 \Gamma_*=(I_C/c)\otimes I_A\otimes(I_B/b)=I_{CAB}/(cb).
\end{equation}
Its realization has moment $\pair{W_\alpha}{\Gamma_*}\geq1$. Thus
$Q_0=a\Tr W_\alpha$ is positive and finite. Equations
\eqref{eq:upper} and \eqref{eq:lower} bound both quantities above by
$\kappa^{-1}\log_2Q_0$.

Let $U_{r_*}$ denote the ordinary supremum with the inserted reference fixed
to $r_*=cab$. The realization of $\Gamma_*$ supplies a finite lower bound
for this supremum, and
$U_{r_*}\leq\U\leq\Adiv\leq\kappa^{-1}\log_2Q_0$.
Hence $U_{r_*}=y$ for some finite real number $y$. Realization of every
positive definite feasible tester gives
\[
 \kappa^{-1}\log_2\pair{W_\alpha}{\Gamma}\leq y,
 \qquad\pair{W_\alpha}{\Gamma}\leq2^{\kappa y}.
\]
To extend this bound to any feasible $\Gamma$, form
$\Gamma_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)\Gamma+\varepsilon\Gamma_*$ with
$0<\varepsilon<1$. It is positive definite and feasible, with positive
definite marginal $(1-\varepsilon)\tau+\varepsilon I_C/c$. The pairing
is affine in $\varepsilon$, so letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives
the same bound for $\Gamma$. This step uses only an affine scalar
expression; no continuity of inverse powers at a singular denominator is
assumed.

Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:upper} with $Q=2^{\kappa y}$ to conclude
$\Adiv\leq y$. Therefore
\[
 y=U_{r_*}\leq\U\leq\Adiv\leq y,
\]
proving collapse and the reference-dimension assertion in the supported case.

If $J_1\not\ll J_2$, realize $\Gamma_*$ instead. Its invertible congruence
gives unsupported ordinary output states, so $\U=+\infty$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:lower} then gives $\Adiv=+\infty$ as well. The lower-bound
test has zero finite input cost, so no subtraction of infinite values occurs.
This proves the theorem for all physical pairs.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite tester characterization]\label{cor:formula}
For $J_1\ll J_2$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
 Q_\alpha=\sup_{\Gamma\ \mathrm{feasible}}
                  \pair{\Tr_D\G(J_1,J_2)}{\Gamma}.
\end{equation}
Then $1\leq Q_\alpha<\infty$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formula}
 \Adiv=\U=U_{cab}=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log_2Q_\alpha.
\end{equation}
For unsupported comb pairs both divergences are $+\infty$, already witnessed
at reference dimension $cab$. The asserted reference bound concerns an
equality of suprema; it makes no claim that an optimizing physical test is
attained.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The proof of the theorem gives a finite $y$ with all tester pairings at most
$2^{\kappa y}$. Any other upper bound $Q'$ is at least the pairing of
$\Gamma_*$, hence $Q'\geq1$. Proposition~\ref{prop:upper} then gives
$y=\Adiv\leq\kappa^{-1}\log_2Q'$, so $2^{\kappa y}\leq Q'$.
Thus $2^{\kappa y}$ is the least upper bound in \eqref{eq:q}.
The unsupported assertion follows from the same realization used above.
\end{proof}
